# Soil Health & Nutrient Management Guide

## 1. N-P-K Soil Nutrients Overview
Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K) form the fundamental macronutrient trio for crop vigor:
- **Nitrogen (N)**: Essential for vegetative growth, chlorophyll synthesis, and deep green foliage. Nitrogen deficiency presents as chlorosis (yellowing) of older lower leaves first. Over-application leads to excessive foliage, lodging, and delayed maturity.
- **Phosphorus (P)**: Crucial for root development, flowering, seed setting, and energy transfer (ATP). Deficiency displays as purplish discoloration along leaf margins and stunted root architectures.
- **Potassium (K)**: Governs stomatal opening/closing, drought tolerance, pest resistance, and carbohydrate translocation. Deficiency causes marginal leaf scorching (tip burn).

## 2. Soil pH and Nutrient Availability
Optimal soil pH for most staple crops ranges between 6.0 and 7.5:
- **Acidic Soils (pH < 6.0)**: Aluminum and manganese toxicity can occur; phosphorus gets fixed as insoluble aluminum/iron phosphates. Neutralize using Agricultural Lime (Calcium Carbonate) or Dolomite at 1-2 tons per acre based on soil test buffer capacity.
- **Alkaline Soils (pH > 7.8)**: Micronutrient deficiencies (Iron, Zinc, Boron) predominate. Apply agricultural gypsum (calcium sulfate) or elemental sulfur along with well-decomposed Farm Yard Manure (FYM).

## 3. Organic Matter & Soil Moisture Conservation
- Incorporate 5-10 tons/hectare of composted Farm Yard Manure or Vermicompost before primary tillage.
- Practice green manuring with Sesbania (Dhaincha) or Sunhemp; incorporate into soil at 45 days prior to flowering.
- Mulching with straw or crop residues lowers soil temperature by 3-5°C and cuts evaporation losses by up to 35%.
